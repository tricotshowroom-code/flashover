INT. DOT'S DINER - DAY

Lunch thinned out. Two booths still working on pie. A
ceiling fan that moves the smell of the grill around and
nothing else.

Behind the counter, DOT MABRY, 60s, flat-footed, forearms
built by decades of handling trays. She pours before she's
asked to.

In the back booth, RUCKER. Hat on the seat beside him. A
file open. Cold cup of coffee in front of him. Dot tops it
off without breaking his concentration.

MERCER comes in, civilian clothes, a hospital bracelet still
on his wrist, and stops inside the door the way a man does
in a place he has never been. He spots Rucker. Crosses to
the booth and stops a step short of it, hands at his sides.

                    MERCER
          Marshal. Captain Sloan said I'd
          find you here.

Rucker turns a page.

                    MERCER
          Marshal.

                    RUCKER
          I heard you.

He reads to the end of something. Then his eyes come off the
page as far as the hospital bracelet, and no farther.

                    RUCKER
          You're his probie.

                    MERCER
          Danny Mercer.

                    RUCKER
          They tell me you kept your
          facepiece sealed.

                    MERCER
          Yes, sir. Academy said keep the
          seal. Better to go into arrest than
          breathe the room. That's what the
          -- my mama asked the nurse what
          arrest meant and the nurse said --

He stops, because that is not what he was asked.

                    RUCKER
          Said what.

                    MERCER
          Said it meant my heart stopped. She
          already knew that. She just wanted
          somebody to say it to her.

Rucker lets that go by. He says the next thing to the page.

                    RUCKER
          Well. That's why you're standing in
          Dot's and not laying out at Shady
          Grove.

Mercer shifts his weight. He has nowhere to put his hands
and leaves them where they are.

                    RUCKER
          The ceiling in that building. Tell
          me the last thing it did before it
          came down.

                    MERCER
          It came down. It let go all at once
          --

                    RUCKER
          Before that.

Rucker waits with his file. He doesn't look up.

Mercer looks at the table, not at him. At the cold cup.

                    MERCER
          It got quiet. Rolling and popping
          the whole way in, then it got
          quiet.

Rucker closes the file. Looks at Mercer for the first time.
Pushes the chair out with his boot.

                    RUCKER
          Fire in a void goes quiet right
          before it shows itself. You had
          four seconds of quiet. You spent
          them moving. Sit down.

Mercer sits on the front of the chair, sore, the bracelet
arm held a little off the table. Dot passes with the pot.
Without asking, she leaves a fresh cup beside Mercer. He
starts to thank her. She's already at the next booth.

                    RUCKER
          They tell me you can't pull hose
          for six weeks.

                    MERCER
          Doctor said six. Chief said we'll
          see.

                    RUCKER
          They also tell me you lost the line
          and your captain had to put a
          Mayday out on you.

                    MERCER
          Yes, sir.

Rucker lets that sit on him. Looks at the bracelet. At the
file under his own hand. He does not like what he is about
to do.

                    RUCKER
          Can you carry a camera without
          losing it.

                    MERCER
          Yes, sir.

                    RUCKER
          Then here's how it goes. You drive
          mine. You carry what I hand you.
          You don't touch anything I haven't
          pointed at. And the main thing, you
          listen twice as much as you talk.
          You start now.

                    MERCER
          Now.

Rucker doesn't answer that. He slides the file across the
table, turns it so it reads right side up to Mercer. Cedar
Street photographs.

Mercer looks at the file, not at him. Puts a hand flat on
the edge of it.

At the front, a check paid, a purse closed. MISS ADELE
FANNIN, 80s, stops beside the booth on her way out, because
it is his booth and she always does. One hand flat on the
table, the other on her purse. She looks at the boy, and at
the bracelet, a while.

                    MISS ADELE
          This your boy, Eldon?

                    RUCKER
          Sloan's.

                    MISS ADELE
          Hm.

She has decided something about him anyway. Then she looks
at the file.

                    MISS ADELE
          Eldon.

Rucker looks at Mercer, not at her. Opens the Cedar Street
file to the first photograph and turns it toward him.

                    RUCKER
          Yes, ma'am.

Miss Adele pats the table once and heads for the door. The
bell jingles. Dot lifts the pot at her without stopping.

Mercer studies the first photograph, then the next. The
bracelet goes under the edge of the table.

Rucker drinks the cold coffee and watches him.
