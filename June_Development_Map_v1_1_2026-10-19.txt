JUNE AND DEENA (the interaction recommended, described, not drafted)
Deena is counting the drawer at the end of the bar with her feet half out of her shoes. June comes in off the call wanting to tell it, and Deena makes her wait ("Hold on. Forty. Hold on."), so June tells it anyway, over the count, and Deena loses her place and has to start the stack again, and says so. That is the first thing we enjoy: June cannot hold news, Deena will not be hurried, and both know it about each other.
June gives it as logistics (the flight, two days, two songs, a man from the label on Friday to see her in a room). Deena, who understands early and finishes accounts wrong, finishes this one: June is moving to Los Angeles. June corrects her to what she knows: four days, two songs, a man at a table, "and then I don't know." Deena has advice ready, begins it ("What you do is --"), hears herself, and stops; her card's processing silence, and she does not replace the advice with another verdict. What she says instead is the surprise: she already has somebody for June's Fridays. She has had them for weeks. June thought she was breaking news; Deena has been expecting her to go since before June decided, and says how she knew (she hums the new ones at the walk-in, or she has been singing the slow set differently, something Deena noticed and June did not). June is surprised and then not: that is what Deena is like, and the scene lets her enjoy it.
Deena's own concerns, present through it: the drawer; the keys, which June produces from her apron pocket because she has been the one keeping them since the third Friday (2.1's keys, paid; June is the person in the building who counts); her feet; and a complaint she enjoys telling about Merle, who will not take the Christmas lights down and says they are not Christmas lights, they are lights (the Master's July lights, given a history). The laugh is that.
June's interest in Deena, once: "You ever miss it?" (the job she left; the Guide's one matter-of-fact mention of the past, not explained). Deena: a practical, comic answer in place of the real one ("I miss the parking."), her card's bypass, and June does not press, because she knows the boundary. Reciprocity: Deena brings out June's honesty about not knowing and her impatience at being advised about a thing already chosen; June brings out Deena's attention (the cover, the humming) and her privacy. What each can assume from history: June assumes Deena will cover her; Deena assumes June will leave; both are right.
The test (enjoyable, revealing time with June without the fire or the opportunity): yes, because the scene is two women closing a bar, one of whom cannot wait and one of whom will not be rushed, with the opportunity as the news of the night and not the subject of the scene.
Ends inside, on the two of them, with the drawer counted and the lights on. Then 2.7 outside.
Page impact: not promised. The Writer's estimate for the whole block, call and closing, is between one and a half and two pages, UNVERIFIED until drafted and formatted.

2.9, the call: June's own agenda (the room Thursday, the new songs for the studio) stopped dead by Hoyt's trouble; the only encounter where she gives and gets nothing; the invitation that puts him in the room. Hoyt here is the one she owes.
