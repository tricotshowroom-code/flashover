EXT. ALDRIDGE DRY GOODS BUILDING - NIGHT

Through a facepiece. The edges of the lens frame everything.
Breathing, close, already going.

Gloved hands come up on both sides of the lens and press.
A thumb runs the seal. Behind the hands, masked, CAPTAIN
CARL SLOAN, 50s, barrel-chested, gray mustache. The man
inside the facepiece is DANNY MERCER, 25, probationary
firefighter, broad-shouldered, young.

                    SLOAN
               (muffled, mask)
          Air?

                    MERCER
               (muffled, mask)
          Full.

Sloan puts the regulator to the mask and it clicks home. He
takes Mercer's shoulder strap in his fist and turns him
toward the building.

A two-story red-brick building on a block of shuttered
storefronts. Smoke pulses from the eaves. No flame visible.
An ENGINE at the corner, EARL DOBBS, 40s, company engineer,
at the pump panel looking up at the roofline. Ladders going
to the roof. The RIT team waiting with tools.

At the command board, the INCIDENT COMMANDER, radio traffic
in both ears.

                    INCIDENT COMMANDER
               (into radio)
          Command to all units, building
          reported vacant, occupancy
          unconfirmed. Engine Three, you're
          interior. Give me conditions when
          you've got them.

Sloan's hand comes off the strap long enough to point at the
hose. The door. The dark past it. They get low and go in.

INT. ALDRIDGE DRY GOODS BUILDING - CONTINUOUS

BLACK.

Breathing. Close, mechanical, air pulled through a
regulator, pushed back out.

Water moving. A pump throbbing far off, through walls.

A helmet lamp comes up: light that reaches three feet.

Gloved hands on a hoseline, following it forward. The line
is the only road in here. Back along it is the door.

The lamp finds the nozzle. Mercer braces behind it. Sloan
keeps one hand on his shoulder strap.

They move on their hands and knees. Heat keeps them there.

                    SLOAN
               (muffled, mask)
          Pencil it.

Mercer opens the nozzle at the overhead, one short burst. It
comes back down on them as hissing steam. They keep moving.

                    MERCER
          Where is it?

                    SLOAN
          Above us or ahead of us. Watch your
          ceiling.

Sloan keys the radio on his chest.

                    SLOAN
               (radio)
          Heavy smoke. High heat. Zero
          visibility. We're penciling.

                    INCIDENT COMMANDER (V.O.)
               (radio, tinny)
          Interior, you in position with a
          charged line?

                    SLOAN
               (radio)
          Charged and on it. It's above us.
          Give us a hole and we'll take it.

EXT. ALDRIDGE DRY GOODS BUILDING - NIGHT

Three firefighters sound the roof ahead of every step with
pike poles.

                    INCIDENT COMMANDER
               (into radio)
          Roof, Command. Open it up.

EXT. ALDRIDGE - ROOF - CONTINUOUS

The saw starts on the third pull. It revs to full power. The
blade bites hard into the roof.

Smoke presses along the cut.

The opening breaks through. Smoke stands up in a hard
column.

INT. ALDRIDGE - CONTINUOUS

The smoke LIFTS a foot off the floor.

The building appears as the draft takes hold: shelving rows.
A dead forklift. An aisle. For the first time, a shape to
the dark.

                    SLOAN
          There's our vent. Move.

They advance up the aisle, the line paying out behind them.

Outside, the engine pump climbs higher, Dobbs feeding them
more pressure.

Ahead, the ceiling GLOWS through its seams.

Fire rolls from the ceiling void, across the overhead,
running for the vent. Above the roof the cut throws flame,
and the roof crew goes flat.

Behind Sloan, a section of ceiling and steel rack lets go.
CRASHES across the hoseline.

The lamp beams disappear into black.

                    SLOAN
          Back it out! Back --

The hose line is pinned beneath the debris. Sloan hauls at
it. Mercer is three feet deeper into the room, cut off from
Sloan by burning shelving. Three feet.

                    MERCER
          Cap --

The VIBRALERT goes off against his jaw. Low air.

                    SLOAN
               (radio)
          MAYDAY MAYDAY MAYDAY. Engine Three.
          Firefighter separated. Northeast
          corner. Low air --

                    INCIDENT COMMANDER (V.O.)
               (radio, tinny, far)
          Command copies the Mayday. RIT is
          deploying.

Mercer, alone.

He drops a glove onto the hoseline and follows it. The line
goes two ways. One way is the nozzle. The other is the door.

Five feet. A coupling. His fingers find the lugs on the far
side. Lugs point to the nozzle. Wrong way. He has been
crawling deeper in. He turns and crawls back along the line.

The regulator gives him less air. The Vibralert pounds,
slows, then stops. He inhales and gets nothing; the
facepiece seal pulls tight against his cheeks.

His gloves grip the edges of the facepiece.

He does not pull it off. He releases it and puts both hands
back on the hoseline.

He crawls. Slower now. Each attempt to inhale pulls the seal
tighter. His helmet light drops toward the floor.

He stops moving.

The beam remains fixed across the floor. Smoke passes
through it.

His PASS device begins to chirp. The warning accelerates,
then rises into a full electronic SHRIEK.

Nothing else moves. Faint traffic comes through the radio on
his chest:

                    SLOAN (V.O.)
               (radio, tinny)
          Mercer. Mercer, key your mic.

Nothing. The shriek. Smoke crossing the beam.

                    RIT OFFICER (V.O.)
               (radio)
          RIT to Command, we're at the
          collapse. No contact. We're
          digging.

                    INCIDENT COMMANDER (V.O.)
               (radio)
          Command to all units, emergency
          traffic only. Three minutes on the
          Mayday.

The beam is dimmer. The smoke is thick and rolling.

                    SLOAN (V.O.)
               (radio, raw)
          He was right beside me. He's on the
          line. He is ON the line --

                    INCIDENT COMMANDER (V.O.)
               (radio)
          RIT, Command. Status.

Static. No answer.

The beam. The smoke. The shriek.

A GLOVED HAND reaches through the smoke, finds Mercer's
shoulder strap and locks on.

                    RIT FIREFIGHTER
               (muffled, close)
          GOT HIM! We got him! GO!

Mercer is YANKED backward. Floorboards rush beneath him. His
lamp swings away from the beam.

EXT. ALDRIDGE - ENTRY DOOR - CONTINUOUS

Two RIT firefighters drag Mercer across the threshold. His
mask is still sealed. His PASS continues to scream until one
of them shuts it off.

Clean air. Concrete apron. Mask off. Helmet off.

A FIREFIGHTER-MEDIC's fingers go to his throat.

                    FIREFIGHTER-MEDIC
          No pulse. Starting compressions.

Coat open. Compressions. Bag-valve mask. AED pads. Behind
them, the building ROARS as master streams open up.

Mercer COUGHS, wet, tearing. Fights the mask on his face.

                    FIREFIGHTER-MEDIC
          Pulse. We got a pulse. Package him.

He keys the mic on his shoulder.

                    FIREFIGHTER-MEDIC
          Command, Medical. I want the
          helicopter.

                    COMMAND (V.O.)
          Say again, Medical.

                    FIREFIGHTER-MEDIC
          Helicopter. He was down and he's
          back. We need to get him to the
          nearest trauma center.

EXT. ALDRIDGE - CLEARING - LATER

Open ground a hundred yards off the fire. The aircraft comes
up off the grass with Mercer inside it. Rotor wash drives
ash flat across the lot. The firefighters under it turn
their backs.

It noses over the tree line. Gone.
