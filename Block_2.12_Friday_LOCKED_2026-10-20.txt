SCENE

INT. THE MAGNOLIA LOUNGE - NIGHT

Warm, loud, low-ceilinged. Christmas lights over the bar in
July. A dance floor the size of a kitchen. A four-piece band
on a bandstand one foot high.

Pete on the door, calling every name coming in.

Deena lands four longnecks on a table mid-story and keeps
moving.

JUNE comes in off the front step past Pete, tucking a pack
into her apron pocket. Pete's look follows her. She drops a
basket at a table, then steps up onto the bandstand still in
her apron and unclips it.

                    DEENA
               (as she goes by)
          One of these days you're gonna have
          to pick. Tray or that guitar.

                    JUNE
          I picked. This is the last tray.

                    DEENA
          Listen to her.

At a corner table, RAY BEASLEY, 70s, lean in a pressed
shirt, holds his hat on one knee while ONDINE, 70s, in a
floral dress, talks with both hands and straightens his
collar.

At the bar, a bottled Coke sweats before an empty stool.
LACEY STROUD, 17, slight, bright red lipstick and a church
dress taken in at the waist, slips in behind a birthday
crowd and claims it.

A man comes in alone. The only sport coat in the room. Pete
holds the door for him and has no name to call. The man goes
to the bar and stands at the back of the crowd with the wall
behind him, where he can see all of it. This is MARTY KEEL.

Deena, going by with a tray, looks at him a second longer
than a stranger gets. Keeps going.

HOYT enters in a clean button-down, empty-handed. Pete's
handshake holds an extra beat.

                    BIG PETE
          You eat?

                    HOYT
          She on yet?

Pete lets go of his hand. Hoyt goes in.

Passing a rocking table, Hoyt crouches and folds a coaster
beneath one leg. As he rises, Ondine catches him by the arm.

                    ONDINE
          Merle ought to be ashamed. Nine
          years, Amos.

                    HOYT
          Ain't my place anymore.

                    ONDINE
          It ain't right, Amos.

                    HOYT
          I came for June.

She lets him go. At the end of the bar, Deena sets a beer in
front of him without being asked. The band finishes tuning.
The drummer counts it in before June has the strap over her
shoulder. The count dies. The room quiets.

June looks once to the back of the room. A man she doesn't
know, at the bar, looking back. She doesn't look there
again.

                    JUNE
               (at the mic)
          Ray and Miss Ondine. Miss Ondine
          has talked me through every bad day
          I ever had, and Ray sat right there
          and let her. Thirty-one years
          married, tonight. This one's
          theirs. And that man still won't
          dance.

                    RAY
               (from his table)
          Can't. Not won't.

People at the tables laugh. Her boot comes down, one, two,
and the band hits. A slow number.

Ondine looks at Ray. Ray holds his hat.

June sings. Full voice now.

The floor. Couples who have been here before, and two who
haven't, finding the step. A table by the wall stops talking
in the middle of something. In the kitchen door, the kid in
the wet apron with a bus tub on one hip, not moving.

Lacey on her stool, the Coke in both hands, watching the
bandstand.

Hoyt at the end of the bar. The beer where Deena put it. He
watches June.

At the back of the crowd, Marty. He has stopped looking at
the room and is looking at her. Deena works the bar in front
of him, pulling longnecks for a tray.

                    MARTY
          Does she know they're here for her?

Deena racks the bottles. Doesn't answer. He waits, then
gives it up and turns back to the floor. To June.

                    DEENA
               (over his shoulder)
          She thinks they come because it's
          Friday. She always has.

He doesn't turn around. He is smiling.

At the door, Pete looks across the packed floor. People are
still coming.

                    BIG PETE
               (half to himself)
          Ain't even payday.

Nobody answers. The floor fills.

June, eyes closed on the long note. Then open, on them. The
couples. Ondine's hand on Ray's hat. The whole room facing
one way.
