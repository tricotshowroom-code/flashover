EXT. THE MAGNOLIA - REAR DOOR - NIGHT

Late. The lot is nearly empty. The rear door stands propped
open on a brick, kitchen light across the pavement.

MERLE COBB, 60s, owner of the Magnolia, reading glasses
pushed up in his hair, on the phone beside the door. A
hardware-store bag at his feet.

                    COBB
               (into phone)
          -- no, bring it in the back. The
          back. ... Monday. Monday's fine.
          ... I said it's fine. Tell Dunphy.

He hangs up.

BIG PETE TILLMAN, 50s, six-four, heavy through the chest and
belly, thick hands, comes out the propped door onto the step
on his round, and stops when he sees Cobb.

                    COBB
          You hear what's going on in town?

                    BIG PETE
          June?

                    COBB
          No. Somebody's burning buildings.

He picks up the bag and hands it to Pete.

                    COBB
          Nobody comes through my back door.

Cobb goes in. Pete follows him.

INT. THE MAGNOLIA - REAR HALL - CONTINUOUS

Cobb kicks the brick out and lets the door swing shut behind
them. Latched. The push bar across it at waist height.

Pete takes a length of chain and a padlock out of the bag,
the price sticker still on it. He looks at the door. Then at
the push bar.

                    BIG PETE
          That's an exit, Merle.

                    COBB
          It's a service door. Ain't nobody
          using that at night.

                    BIG PETE
          It's got a bar on it.

Cobb says nothing.

Pete holds up the chain.

                    BIG PETE
          Are we even allowed? I don't think
          we can chain that door.

                    COBB
          I ain't risking somebody walking in
          here and burning this place down in
          the middle of the night.

From the barroom, down the hall, a burst of laughter. Music
underneath it. Pete looks that way.

                    BIG PETE
          Every night?

                    COBB
          Every night. Once the kitchen's
          closed.

Pete runs the chain through the push bar and the door frame
and snugs it tight. He closes the padlock.

SNAP.

Cobb takes the key and threads it onto his key ring. Pete
glances at the chained door.

Music, voices and bottles clinking come down the hall.

Cobb pockets the key ring.
