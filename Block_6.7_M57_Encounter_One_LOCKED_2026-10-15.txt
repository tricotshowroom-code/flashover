INT. HOLMAN - VISITATION - DAY

A SERGEANT, 50s, checks Vance's credentials, then Rucker's.
He straightens.

                      SERGEANT
          Marshal Rucker. My daddy rode an
          engine in Selma. He'd talk about
          you.

                      RUCKER
          What company?

                      SERGEANT
          Engine Two.

                      RUCKER
          Good company.

The sergeant hands the identification back with both hands.
Rucker empties his pockets into the tray.

Booths. Cloudy glass, palm marks at hand height. A cartoon-
dog sticker on the middle pane. Rucker sits there. Vance
takes the chair behind him, legal pad open.

The far door. AMOS HOYT, 50s, leaner, his whites washed
gray, stops at the painted line and looks at who is on the
other side. The OFFICER nods him forward. He stays at the
line a while longer. Then he crosses and sits. The receiver
stays on its hook.

Rucker lifts his. Waits.

Hoyt looks at Vance's blank pad. Then he picks up.

                      HOYT
          You went gray.

                      RUCKER
          Yes.

                      HOYT
          It suit you.

Rucker does not know what to do with that. Hoyt watches him
not know.

                      VANCE
          Amos, we talked about how this
          would go. The Marshal has questions
          about the storeroom, and you said
          --

                      HOYT
          I can tell him what I agreed to.

                      VANCE
          Then tell him.

                      HOYT
                (to Rucker)
          I agreed to sit down.

                      RUCKER
          All right.

                      HOYT
          That's all I agreed to.

                      RUCKER
          I heard you.

                      HOYT
          Twelve years. You don't come once.
          You don't write. You don't send her
          so much as a --

He stops. Not because he is finished.

                      RUCKER
          Go on.

                      HOYT
          Nineteen people, Marshal. You don't
          seem tore up.

Rucker reaches down for the folder.

                      HOYT
          That ain't an answer.

                      RUCKER
          No.

He sets the frame 61 enlargement against the glass.

                      RUCKER
          This is the storeroom. Frame sixty-
          one. The other nine stop at that
          corner --

                      HOYT
          That's my door.

                      RUCKER
          -- this one doesn't.

                      HOYT
          I wired that door open.
          Compressor'd cook itself in there
          with it shut. You can see the wire.

                      RUCKER
          I can see it now.

                      HOYT
          Now.

Rucker's thumb on the edge of the photograph.

                      HOYT
          What else is in the folder.

Rucker lowers the photograph. An old legal-pad sheet against
the glass. Dates in a column, in his hand.

                      RUCKER
          The county's fires. The weeks
          between them.

                      HOYT
          Who wrote this.

                      RUCKER
          I did.

                      HOYT
          When.

                      RUCKER
          The summer we arrested you.

Hoyt reads it. Reads it again.

                      HOYT
          Six weeks. Four. Three. Six to
          mine. Then what.

                      RUCKER
          Then nothing.

                      HOYT
          Nothing while I was in county.
          Nothing while I was at trial.

                      RUCKER
          No.

                      HOYT
          You stood up at my trial.

                      RUCKER
          I did.

                      HOYT
          With this in your --

                      RUCKER
          It was in the file.

                      HOYT
          In the file.

                      RUCKER
          It was in the --

                      HOYT
          I heard you. I'm saying it back so
          you hear it.

Hoyt puts the receiver down flat on the counter. Rucker can
hear him breathe through it. Then Hoyt picks it back up.

                      RUCKER
          I need the storeroom. The Friday.
          What was under the west shelves.

                      HOYT
          Chaplain keeps me a list. Who got
          buried when. I know who sang at
          Deena's. I got nineteen of those.
          You asked me a question, that's
          what I keep under my shelves.

                      RUCKER
          I can't use that.

                      HOYT
          No. You can't.

                      RUCKER
          Amos --

                      HOYT
          No.

Rucker stops.

                      HOYT
          My girl was sixteen when they took
          me out my yard. She's a grown woman
          now. When that date comes, the one
          thing the State of Alabama hands
          her is a paper says her daddy
          burned nineteen people alive. That
          paper outlives me. You're the one
          man whose name can --

He stops. His hand has closed on the counter.

                      RUCKER
          I --

                      HOYT
          Ask me about the foam.

                      RUCKER
          The foam. That Friday.

                      HOYT
          It was a storeroom twelve years
          ago.

                      RUCKER
          Don't try to remember it. Stand in
          it. You come in off the rear hall.
          Cooler's at your back.

                      HOYT
          Cooler's on my right. You come in
          off the hall, cooler's on your
          right, door swings left into the
          mop sink.

                      RUCKER
                (writing)
          On your right.

                      HOYT
          Then the crates. Beer crates, three
          high.

                      RUCKER
          What's under the shelves.

                      HOYT
          The man running the crew. I told
          him twice I had to get in to the
          compressor Monday. He said it's
          temporary. Everything's temporary
          to a man don't have to get in there
          Monday.

                      RUCKER
          The foam, Hoyt.

                      HOYT
          I'm telling you the foam. You asked
          what's under the shelves, I'm
          telling you how it got there.
          Rolled. On end. Up against my
          shelving. Three deep.

                      RUCKER
          Three deep under the shelves, or
          out in the --

                      HOYT
          Under. I said under. They kept
          bringing it in off the truck till
          you couldn't walk a line to the
          compressor. Three deep. Packed.

                      RUCKER
          Anybody open a roll. Move the
          stack.

                      HOYT
          Not while I was there.

                      RUCKER
          There's a jug. On the sill.

Hoyt closes his eyes.

                      HOYT
          Five gallon. Two inches of water in
          it. Crew was about to kick it over.
          I set it up there myself.

He opens his eyes. He is somewhere else for a second.

                      HOYT
          I was keeping their mess off my
          floor.

                      RUCKER
                (writing)
          Day before.

                      HOYT
          Day before.

Hoyt watches the pen.

                      HOYT
          Say you find it.

                      RUCKER
          I don't know that I will.

                      HOYT
          I didn't ask what you know. Say you
          find it. What happens to you.

                      RUCKER
          That's not what this is for.

                      HOYT
          It's what I'm asking.

Rucker does not answer. Hoyt reads the answer off him
anyway.

                      HOYT
          You had a Black man with keys and a
          grudge. That was room enough.

Rucker does not answer that, and does not look away from it.

                      HOYT
          Here's mine. You put your name on
          it. Not hers. Yours. And it goes to
          Montgomery. You make the State
          receive it.

                      VANCE
          Amos --

                      HOYT
          I'm talking to him.

                      VANCE
          And I'm talking to you. The day he
          signs doubt without cause they pull
          him off it, and your date stands. I
          don't need you innocent. I need the
          cause to fail.

                      HOYT
                (to Rucker, not to her)
          Then get the cause. Then do what I
          said. I'm done trusting what stays
          in a file.

                      RUCKER
          I --

                      OFFICER
          Time.

Hoyt stands before Rucker can finish. He hangs the receiver
on its hook and walks to the door without looking back.

Rucker stays seated, the phone still in his hand.
