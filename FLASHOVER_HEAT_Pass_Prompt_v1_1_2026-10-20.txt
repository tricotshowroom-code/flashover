FLASHOVER — HEAT PASS PROMPT — v1.1 — 20 October 2026 (H16 added from the Judge's reading of 2.9 Draft 03)
Paste this block after the Human Realism Dialogue Prompt v2.7 and its gate, as the final pass on every scene before it is returned. It does not replace v2.7; it runs after it, on the scene v2.7 has passed. Source: the dialogue patterns of HEAT (Michael Mann, revised draft 3 March 1994), read for how people speak, who carries the information and what the others do about it. Nothing in this prompt is wording to borrow. The Judge's two real-speech samples (the 4 a.m. drive; the sports-radio fragment of 20 Oct) remain the rhythm standard.

1. INPUT
TASK: HEAT PASS on [block / draft number]. The scene as it passed v2.7. The cast cards. The exchange map and tally from the v2.7 gate.

2. THE FIFTEEN RULES (each from a pattern in the source; the pattern is stated so the rule is not taken on faith)
H1. OWNER AND MOVE. In the source, information always has an owner and the owner dominates; the other person never becomes a second explainer. For each exchange, name who owns the information and classify what the other does: ACCEPT (a word); CUT (at the first sentence); TERMS (a why answered with conditions or a price); REFUSE (will not discuss it); DECIDE (silence, then a decision). An exchange in which the other merely asks the next question so that the owner can keep going is a FEED LINE and FAILS, unless the person has a reason of his own to feed him, stated.
H2. LENGTH IS POSITION. The one who needs something talks more; the one who decides talks less, often in a word. If the decider is explaining, he is selling, and the page must show whom he is selling to. If he is not selling, cut him to the terms.
H3. NO TWO CARRIERS. Two consecutive speeches that both deliver information FAIL, whoever speaks them. One becomes a reply.
H4. THE SHORT SIDEWAYS LINE. Every speech over three sentences is followed by a short reply that deflates, redirects or gives a number, or the pass states why this listener, in this state, would not.
H5. THE RETURNED PROBE. A question may be answered in the asker's own grammar, or with terms; the returned probe is the answer and nobody explains it. Zero instances in a scene need a reason per question.
H6. THE ENDING. A scene ends on an instruction, a price, a one-word acceptance, or a flat fact. A summation FAILS. A line that tells the audience what the scene was FAILS.
H7. THE REAL LINE. Where a scene has one, it arrives after a deflection and a physical act, and gets a reply of a word or nothing. A real line delivered cold, or answered with a speech, FAILS.
H8. THE NAMED ROUTINE. In a familiar pair, one may name the other's routine before it is performed; that is history on the page. If the pair has history and neither names anything, say why.
H9. NUMBERS. Every number in dialogue carries something the speaker will not say, or it is exposition. Exposition numbers are cut or moved to a document or a sign.
H10. THE ECHO. A word repeated from the other's mouth is a challenge; the owner of the word must answer the echo, not his own question. An echo that is only confirmation FAILS unless the person is confirming for a reason.
H11. SPLIT ATTENTION. On a call, or across a room, the page says where the eyes are, and they may not be on the subject of the words.
H12. THE EXPLAINER'S POSITION. The character who explains most is the weaker one in the scene. If the strong one explains, H2 applies.
H13. SPEED. Pressure slows and repeats; nerves speed up and list. A nervous character in measured sentences FAILS; a man in control who lists FAILS.
H14. OBEYED IN ACTION. An order's reply may be the doing of it. Do not write a verbal acknowledgment where the body would answer.
H15. ONE MOUTH. A speaker thinking aloud may lose a sentence and go on without repairing it, or answer his own question. Do not hand the repair to the listener by reflex.
H16. THE PREOCCUPIED LISTENER (added 20 Oct from 2.9 Draft 03, the Judge's reading). One person is excited and runs: she drifts mid-sentence, realizes she told somebody else, and starts her story from the top. The other has his own thing on his mind and it is the only thing there; her story is the last thing he can hear, so he does not answer it, he says his thing. Test every exchange for this: does the listener have his own weather, and if he does, does it break through regardless of what the speaker is saying? A listener with something of his own who waits politely for the speaker to finish FAILS. The map must show the speaker's drift and self-correction in one mouth (H15) and the listener's eruption as WRONG SUBJECT by necessity, with the silence after it supported by the speaker having been mid-story.

3. THE PASS
STEP A. Reread the scene as it passed v2.7. Do not alter it yet.
STEP B. HEAT TALLY. For H1, list each exchange with its owner and the other's move; count FEED LINES. For H2 and H12, name who needs and who decides and which talks more. For H3, list any pair. For H4, list each speech over three sentences and the line that follows it. For H5, list returned probes, or the reason per question. For H6, quote the ending and its type. For H7, quote the real line, what precedes it and what follows. For H8, name any routine named, or why none. For H9, list every number and what it carries. For H10, list echoes and what they do. For H11, say where the eyes are on any call. For H13, name any run of clauses and the state it matches. For H14, list orders and how they are answered. For H15, list any one-mouth repair or self-answer. For H16, name each listener's own weather, if any, and where it breaks through; a listener who has something of his own and waits is a failure.
STEP C. VERDICT. PASS, REVISE or BLOCKED. REVISE is the default where H1 finds a feed line without a reason, H3 finds a pair, H6 finds a summation, or H7 finds the real line delivered cold. BLOCKED only where a governing source (a lock, a blueprint line, a Guide requirement) prevents the repair; name the conflict.
STEP D. REPAIR. Smallest change, in the person's own grammar, never in the source's wording. Rerun v2.7's gate on the changed passage, then rerun this pass on the whole scene. Repeat until no identifiable violation remains. Record every change in the change note with the rule that required it.
STEP E. RETURN. The HEAT TALLY and verdict, before the scene. Then the scene, labelled as v2.7 requires. Then the change note.

4. WHAT THIS PASS NEVER DOES
It never borrows a line, a phrase or a cadence from the source. It never inserts a behavior to obtain a tally; a rule that does not bite is reported as not biting, with the reason in the person. It never overrides v2.7, the canon, the blueprint, the Guide or a lock; where it would, it reports BLOCKED and names the conflict. It never alters a LOCKED block; findings on a locked block are proposed amendments for the Judge. It does not certify a scene it has not read in full after the last repair.
